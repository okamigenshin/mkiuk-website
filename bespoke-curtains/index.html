Bespoke curtains in Mill Hill

Made-to-measure curtains, from the first sample to the final fitting.

Fabric, lining and light

Start with how you want the room to feel and how much light you want to keep. Linen and cotton offer different textures; velvet gives a richer surface, while sheer fabrics soften daylight. The weave, weight and composition affect the chosen fabric’s drape and care requirements.

Discuss unlined, lined or interlined curtains alongside your privacy and light-control needs. A blackout lining can help reduce light through the fabric, but the finished result also depends on gaps around the window and the fitting.

Headings, tracks and window shape

Pencil pleat, pinch pleat and wave-style headings create different folds and need compatible hardware. Curtain fullness, stack-back space and the position of the track or pole affect how much glass is exposed when the curtains are open.

For bay windows, wide openings or floor-length curtains, bring photographs and approximate dimensions to your first conversation. Final measurements and fixing positions need to be checked before making.

Designed, made and fitted

Our Mill Hill showroom and in-house workshop bring the design, making and fitting stages together. We work with private clients, interior designers and hotels. Tell us about your room, fabric preferences and practical requirements so we can discuss a suitable specification.

Common questions

How do I choose curtain fabric?

Compare samples in the room where the curtains will hang. Consider colour, texture, drape, light exposure and the care instructions, then discuss lining and heading options with our team.

Can curtains reduce light in a bedroom?

Blackout lining can reduce light through the fabric. Track position, overlaps and gaps at the sides, top and bottom also affect the result, so discuss the complete fitting rather than the lining alone.
